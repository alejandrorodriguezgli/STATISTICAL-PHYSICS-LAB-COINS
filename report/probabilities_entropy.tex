\section{Probabilities, Entropy, and Thermodynamics}
\subsection{Parameter Assignment}

We consider a system of $N=100+M$ unbiased coins, where $M$ is the harmonic mean of the last two digits of the team members' NIAs (46, 13, 28 and 06).

\[
M=\frac{4}{\frac{1}{46}+\frac{1}{13}+\frac{1}{28}+\frac{1}{6}}
\approx 13.2871
\]

Therefore, $N \approx 113.2871$. Since $N$ must be an integer, we choose $N=114$, the nearest even integer, so that $N/2=57$ is also an integer. 


\subsection{Probability Calculations}

The probability of obtaining $k$ heads is:

\[
P_k=\frac{\Omega(N,k)}{2^N}
=\frac{N!}{k!(N-k)!2^N}
\]

For $N=114$, the calculated probabilities are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
\hline
Part & Macrostate or interval & Probability \\
\hline
(a) & 30 heads & $1.3931\times10^{-7}$ \\
(b) & 57 heads & $0.074565$ \\
(c) & 47--67 heads & $0.951275$ \\
(d) & 37--77 tails & $0.999896$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For parts (c) and (d), the probabilities are calculated by summing $P_k$ over the corresponding intervals.

The results show that macrostates near $N/2$ are more probable, while states far from the center have very low probabilities.


\subsection{Entropy Changes}

Using Boltzmann's equation,

\[
S=k_B\ln\Omega
\]

the entropy difference is:

\[
\Delta S=S(k_1)-S(k_2)
=k_B\ln\left(\frac{\Omega_1}{\Omega_2}\right)
\]

where $k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}$ J/K.

The results are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
\hline
Part & Macrostates ($k_1$, $k_2$) & $\Delta S$ (J/K) \\
\hline
(e) & (82, 57) & $-1.5519\times10^{-22}$ \\
(f) & (67, 57) & $-2.4133\times10^{-23}$ \\
(g) & (57, 20) & $+3.5628\times10^{-22}$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Entropy is maximum at $k=N/2$, where the multiplicity is largest. The negative values in (e) and (f) indicate lower entropy than the central macrostate, while the positive value in (g) indicates higher entropy.
